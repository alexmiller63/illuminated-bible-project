Footnotes

¹ Bara means “created.” The verb itself does not specify creation from nothing, creatio ex nihilo. Whether Genesis teaches creation ex nihilo is a larger interpretive and theological question, not part of the lexical meaning of bara.

² Shamayim — dual-skies hypothesis. Shamayim has a form historically associated with the Hebrew dual. The Illuminated Bible proposes as a hypothesis that this may be understood as “dual skies”: the sky above and the sky below, together comprising the celestial sphere, with the observer’s horizon dividing the sphere into an upper and a lower hemisphere. This is an interpretive hypothesis, not a proposed translation of shamayim, and the grammatical form alone does not establish it. Genesis 1:8 is particularly significant because the text says that God calls the raqia shamayim. Cross-references: compare chug, “circle/circuit,” in Isaiah 40:22 and Job 26:10, and the imagery of Job 26:7. These passages are offered as parallels for consideration, not as proof of the hypothesis.

³ Ruach Elohim. Ruach can mean “spirit,” “wind,” or “breath.” Accordingly, ruach Elohim has a range of possible resonance broader than the English “God’s Spirit.” “Breath of God” reflects an important sense of ruach, though it is not necessarily the translation intended here.

⁴ Yehi or, vayehi or. Yehi or, vayehi or is remarkably terse and poetic. The repeated or, “light,” and the closely balanced verbal forms give the Hebrew a compactness difficult to reproduce in English. “Shine—and shine was” is not intended as a translation of the Hebrew, but only as an illustration of its concision, repetition, and poetic force.

⁵ Yom echad. Yom echad is literally closer to “one day” or “day one” than to the ordinary Hebrew ordinal “first.”

⁶ Raqia. Raqia is the expanse separating the waters below from the waters above. Genesis 1:8 says that God calls the raqia shamayim, “sky” or “heavens.” The precise physical conception represented by the term is debated.

Editorial Principle

Distinguish what the original-language text says from later interpretations, doctrines, and debates. Illuminate the language first; allow interpretation to follow.
