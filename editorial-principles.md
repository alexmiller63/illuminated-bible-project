# Editorial Principles

The Illuminated Bible is built on a simple principle:

The English translation remains Scripture on the page. Illumination adds information from the original biblical languages without rewriting the translation.

---

## The English Text

Each illuminated verse uses either the **Bible in Basic English (BBE)** or the **World English Bible (WEB)**.

Each verse is considered independently. The editor selects whichever rendering is clearer, stronger, more expressive, or otherwise better suited to the purposes of the Illuminated Bible.

All else being equal, the BBE is preferred.

The source of each illuminated verse is identified by a small **BBE** or **WEB** label.

### The Translation Is Preserved

Once a translation has been selected, its English wording is preserved exactly.

Illumination does not rewrite, correct, harmonize, or modernize the selected translation.

---

## The WEB as the Continuous Reading Text

The right column presents the **World English Bible** in canonical order.

This gives the reader a complete English text that can be read continuously even when an illuminated verse in the left column uses the BBE.

The two columns therefore have different purposes:

**The right column is for reading.**

**The left column is for illumination.**

---

## What Illumination Means

Illumination is linguistic rather than pictorial.

Selected biblically significant words or phrases within the English verse are expanded to reveal important information from the original biblical language.

A typical illumination may include:

- the original Hebrew or Greek

- a transliteration

- pronunciation in the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA)

- a concise explanation of the term's significance

Illumination annotates the translation. It does not replace it.

---

## What Gets Illuminated

Words and phrases are selected because of their biblical significance, not merely because they are nouns, verbs, concrete words, or frequently occurring words.

A word or phrase may warrant illumination when:

- it is a divine name or title;

- it is central to an important biblical or theological concept;

- the original-language term conveys meaning or nuance reduced or obscured in English;

- it has unusual or significant grammatical features;

- it has canonical importance elsewhere in Scripture;

- it functions as a traditional title or name of a biblical book or section;

- it participates in an important recurring biblical motif;

- it provides useful cultural or historical information;

- it provides insight into the worldview or conceptual framework of the biblical writer;

- it forms an important conceptual pair with another term; or

- a translation choice conceals an important feature of the original-language expression.

Illumination should add understanding rather than clutter. Not every English word for which an original-language equivalent can be supplied should be illuminated.

---

## Compound Expressions

When biblical or linguistic significance belongs to a compound expression, the expression is illuminated as a unit rather than artificially divided into separate word illuminations.

---

## Every Verse Stands on Its Own

Every verse must be capable of serving as a reader's first encounter with the Illuminated Bible.

An otherwise warranted illumination is not omitted merely because the same word or expression was illuminated in an earlier verse.

---

## Translation and Original-Language Nuance

An illuminated original-language term may differ in number, grammatical form, semantic range, or nuance from its English rendering.

Those differences belong in the illumination and explanatory material, not in alterations to the selected English translation.

The English text remains the selected translation.

The illumination lets the reader see what lies behind it.
