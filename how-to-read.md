Yes. I recovered an actual earlier version rather than reconstructing it. The saved prototype called the section “HOW TO READ AN ILLUMINATED VERSE,” and the wording was:

“How to Read an Illuminated Verse”

“1 Read the reference.

2 Note the source label (WEB or BEB).

3 Read the illuminated verse. Hebrew or Greek appears only where it illuminates meaning.

4 Read the WEB foundation verse.

5 Consult the footnotes when you want to explore further.

6 Let the Word remain at the center. Illumination serves, it does not replace.”

There was also accompanying methodology text:

“Each illuminated verse is based on either the World English Bible (WEB) or the Berean English Bible (BEB).”

“Hebrew and Greek words appear inline only when they illuminate the English.”

“Illumination Components: Hebrew/Greek Script • Transliteration • English.”

“Footnotes are used only for material that cannot naturally fit into the verse itself.”

“The English remains natural and readable. Illumination never obscures the text.”

This is genuinely recovered material, but it predates several decisions we’ve made since then. In particular, BEB has since become BBE, IPA has been added, the left/right-column architecture has matured, and our treatment of Aramaic and Syriac is now more precise.

So I would preserve this recovered version first, just as we did with the Preface, and then revise it.
